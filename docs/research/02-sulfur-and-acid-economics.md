# Sulfur and Sulfuric Acid Supply, Prices and On-Site Acid Production Economics: Brazil (Minas Gerais) vs Brownsville, Texas

Research date: 4 November 2026. All prices are US$ per metric tonne (t) unless marked otherwise. "lt" means long ton (1 lt = 1.016 t).

Terms used throughout:
- **FOB (free on board):** price at the loading port, with freight not included.
- **CFR (cost and freight):** price delivered to the buyer's port, with ocean freight included.
- **Contract price:** a negotiated monthly or quarterly price. **Spot price:** a single cargo traded now.
- **Sulfur burner / sulfur-burning acid plant:** burns elemental sulfur to SO2 (sulfur dioxide), converts it over a catalyst to SO3 (sulfur trioxide), then absorbs it into acid. Usually built as "DCDA" (double contact, double absorption).
- **HRS (heat recovery system):** recovers absorption heat as low-pressure steam.
- **HPAL (high-pressure acid leach):** a nickel laterite process that uses a lot of acid.
- **SSP / MAP / DAP:** phosphate fertilizers (single superphosphate, mono- and di-ammonium phosphate). They are the main users of acid in Brazil.
- **Smelter acid:** acid recovered from copper or zinc smelter off-gas, as a by-product.
- **Regen acid:** spent refinery alkylation acid regenerated by companies like Ecovyst.

## 1. Chemistry and engineering facts: acid yield per tonne of sulfur, acid-plant capex and opex, by-product steam and power, lead time, minimum economic size, mining-project examples

### Takeaway
One tonne of sulfur makes about 3.0–3.06 t of H2SO4 (100% basis), so sulfur is roughly 0.33 t per t of acid. Sulfur usually makes up 70–80% of the cost of making acid. A sulfur burner also produces about 1.4 t of high-pressure steam per t of acid, enough for about 340 kWh gross (about 260 kWh net) of power per t of acid.

Public capex data points (adjusted for size and date) suggest roughly **US$50–70 M for a plant of about 150–200 kt/yr (about 450–600 t/d)**. A 1,650–2,000 t/d plant has been reported at US$50–110 M depending on country and year. Build times run about 18–30 months. Plants of about 450 t/d are built and considered viable by mining companies; Marimaca is pursuing one in Chile.

### Cited Findings
- **Stoichiometry (inference from atomic weights; no external source):** S (32.06) → H2SO4 (98.08) gives 3.06 t acid per t S, or 0.327 t S per t acid. A paper's feedstock cost line implies about 0.33 t S per t acid, consistent with this. [JSAIMM 2009, "Burning pyrites compared to sulphur" (derived)](https://scielo.org.za/pdf/jsaimm/v109n8/05.pdf)
- **Reference plant, 2,000 t/d sulfur burner on a US site (paper published 2009; cost-basis year not stated):** [JSAIMM 2009](https://scielo.org.za/pdf/jsaimm/v109n8/05.pdf)
  - Capital investment US$90 M. A comparable pyrite-roasting plant was US$150 M.
  - Operating cost excluding feedstock US$10.50/t acid: utilities $3.90, labour $2.70, maintenance $3.90.
  - Power credit –$12.90/t.
  - Total direct production cost $14.30/t at a sulfur price of about $50/t.
- **Steam and power from a sulfur burner (same paper; per-tonne values derived from its tables):** [JSAIMM 2009](https://scielo.org.za/pdf/jsaimm/v109n8/05.pdf)
  - About 116.6 t/h of high-pressure steam at 40 barg and 400 °C, or about 1.4 t steam per t acid.
  - 28.5 MW gross (about 342 kWh/t acid) and 21.5 MW net after site use (about 258 kWh/t).
  - A heat recovery system adds about 6 MW (about 72 kWh/t, or about 0.5 t low-pressure steam per t acid at up to 10 barg).
- **Sulfur share of acid cost:** 70–80% (vendor blog citing market sources, August 2026). [Alliance Chemical](https://alliancechemical.com/blogs/news/sulfuric-acid-plant-curtailments-2026-catalyst-restart-cost)

### Inferences
- **Tiros-scale plant size.** The PEA (preliminary economic assessment) design acid is 186,986 t/yr of 96% acid, about 180 kt/yr on a 100% basis (from the team's PEA extraction notes, `/home/claude/research_notes/Tiros acid split research/pea_data_extraction.md`, Table 17.4). That is about **520–550 t/d** at roughly 330–345 operating days. The 50–300 kt/yr range in the brief corresponds to about 150–900 t/d.
- **Capex estimate.** Scaling Marimaca's US$50–60 M (150 kt/yr, 2025) by the common "0.6 power rule" (a textbook rule of thumb, not sourced here) gives about **US$56–67 M for 180 kt/yr**. It gives about US$33–40 M for 75 kt/yr and about US$85–100 M for 300 kt/yr, before any Brazil or Texas location factor. These are order-of-magnitude (±40%) estimates, not quotes.
- **Capital charge.** At a 10% discount rate over 20 years (capital recovery factor about 0.1175), a US$56–67 M plant costs about **US$37–44 per t of acid** at 180 kt/yr. The figure roughly doubles per tonne at about 50 kt/yr. Capital charge is the main reason small plants (≤300 t/d) only pay off when sulfur-versus-acid spreads are wide or supply security has high value.
- **Non-sulfur operating cost.** Inflating JSAIMM's US$10.50/t (2,000 t/d) and allowing for a smaller plant's higher labor share gives an assumed **US$15–30/t acid** excluding sulfur.
- **Power credit.** A Tiros-size plant would generate about 7.8 MW gross and about 5.9 MW net (342 and 258 kWh/t × about 545 t/d ÷ 24). The PEA's site power demand is only about 3 MW (PEA extraction notes). So a sulfur burner would make the site power self-sufficient with a surplus to export. At US$50–100/MWh the credit is about **US$13–26/t acid**.
- **Steam use.** The steam could also feed acid evaporation and concentration in the regeneration circuit. It cannot supply the 600–680 °C heat for thermal regeneration.
- **Lead time.** Coromandel took 18 months, and the Kazakh and Capstone projects took about 2–3+ years. Suggested planning range: **18–30 months** from FID (final investment decision) to acid production for a package plant, plus permitting.

### Gaps
- No public, current (2025–2026) US or Brazil EPC (engineering, procurement and construction) price for a 300–1,000 t/d sulfur burner was found. The Intratec and IHS/PEP reports are paywalled.
- The scaling exponent used is a textbook rule of thumb, not a sourced acid-plant-specific value.
- No acid-plant capacity or capex was found for Iluka Eneabba, Serra Verde (Brazil) or Arafura Nolans. Ambatovy's plant capacity was not found in an accessible source.
- The 2009 JSAIMM cost-basis year is not stated.

## 2. Prices: sulfur and sulfuric acid, 2020–2026 (highs, lows, current), the 2022 spike and the 2025–2026 crisis

### Takeaway
Sulfur went from very cheap (2023–early 2025: about US$60–180/t) to all-time records in mid-2026 (Brazil CFR about US$1,200; Middle East contract US$1,000; Tampa US$705/lt). The causes were Russia's export restrictions (from 1 Nov 2025), the closure of the Strait of Hormuz (from about 28 Feb–1 Mar 2026), China's halt of acid exports (from May 2026), and Kazakhstan's sulfur export suspension (27 June 2026).

By late October 2026 prices had eased but remained about 3–5× their 2023–2025 levels: Brazil CFR spot US$860–880, US Gulf FOB US$820–830, Middle East FOB US$740–790, Tampa Q4 US$615/lt. Acid peaked at about US$451/t delivered Chile (record) and about US$385–400 in the US Gulf. In Aug–Sep 2026 it was about US$350–390 CFR in Brazil, Chile and the US Gulf. CRU forecasts Chile acid at about US$215 in Q4 2027.

The 2022 spike was much smaller (sulfur about US$470–525/t in May 2022) and reversed within a year.

### Cited Findings — What drove the moves

**2021–2022 spike:** uneven post-Covid recovery, Middle East supply cuts, China's fertilizer export ban, US import restrictions, demand from metals, and Russian supply limits. [Argus, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2851577-tampa-3q-liquid-sulphur-price-hits-record-705-lt)

### Inferences
- **Acid tracks sulfur in a crisis.** Acid follows sulfur once smelter by-product acid and Chinese exports are scarce (S&P says acid is "increasingly tracking elemental sulfur"). At Brazil sulfur of US$870–1,075, the sulfur content of 1 t of acid costs US$285–350, roughly the US$350–370 CFR Brazil acid price. In calm years (2023–2025), acid was cheaper than its own sulfur-plus-conversion cost, because smelter and Chinese by-product acid set the price.
- **Suggested price scenarios for modelling (US$/t):**

| Scenario | Sulfur, Brazil CFR | Tampa delivered | Acid, Brazil CFR | Acid, US Gulf CFR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Low / 2023–mid-2025 | 150–320 | 115–270/lt | 120–130 | 115–155 |
| (b) Pre-war 2026 | about 525 | about 310/lt | about 150–190 (inferred) | about 155 |
| (c) Current, late Oct 2026 | 860–880 spot; about 1,036 contract | 615/lt | about 350–360 | about 345–365 |
| (d) Crisis peak, mid-2026 | 1,200 | 705/lt | about 370+ | about 400 |
| (e) CRU 2027 normalization (Chile acid) | — | — | — | about 215 |

- **Price risk.** In 2020–2026, sulfur's range was more than 10× from trough to peak (US$46 average in 2024 vs US$1,200 CFR Brazil in mid-2026). Any acid-cost model needs a stochastic or scenario sulfur price, not a single point.

### Gaps

- No accessible source covered Brazil acid CFR or US Gulf acid in Oct–Nov 2026; the latest found is early Sep 2026.

Moving rock wins only if Texas offers other large advantages: cheaper sulfur (see §3), power, regeneration energy, product logistics, or incentives.

## 6. Acid recycling and regeneration: sulfate-route TiO2 practice, how regeneration works and costs, relevance to Tiros

### Takeaway
Sulfate-route TiO2 pigment plants use 2.4–3.5 t of concentrated H2SO4 per t TiO2 and produce 6–9 t of spent acid (about 20–22% H2SO4 plus metal sulfates) per t of pigment. Best practice is to evaporate the spent acid to about 70%, crystallize and filter out the iron and metal sulfate salts, and recycle the acid. The salts are then roasted at 800–1,000 °C to SO2 for a contact acid plant, with iron oxide as a by-product. The concentration and roasting energy is about 6–15 GJ per t TiO2.

Tiros' PEA assumes an untested 600–680 °C "acid regeneration" step with no stated recycle percentage. No public cost per tonne of regenerated acid was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Sulfate process figures:** 2.4–3.5 t of concentrated H2SO4 per t TiO2; about 6–9 t of spent acid per t of pigment at about 20–22% H2SO4. Recycling steps: [Umweltbundesamt (German EPA) BAT reference](https://www.umweltbundesamt.de/sites/default/files/medien/publikation/long/3226.pdf)
  - evaporate to about 70%;
  - cool, crystallize and filter the salts, then return the acid to digestion;
  - roast the sulfate filter salts at 800–1,000 °C with sulfur, coal or oil as fuel, which gives SO2 for new acid and iron oxide used by cement makers.
- **German practice:** KRONOS sends spent acid to a Sachtleben recovery plant, and acid is reused at 70% and 80% strength. [UBA](https://www.umweltbundesamt.de/sites/default/files/medien/publikation/long/3226.pdf)
- **Energy for acid concentration plus salt decomposition:** about 5.93–15.17 GJ per t TiO2 (electricity 0.13–1.3, steam 0–5.07, coal possibly 5.8–8.5). The source table layout makes the coal attribution unclear. [UBA](https://www.umweltbundesamt.de/sites/default/files/medien/publikation/long/3226.pdf)
- **Tiros PEA assumptions (team's extraction notes):** [PEA extraction notes](/home/claude/research_notes/Tiros%20acid%20split%20research/pea_data_extraction.md)
  - Regeneration "assumed in the process design … approximately 600 °C, producing an SO3-bearing gas … hydrated to regenerate concentrated H2SO4 … conceptual and untested." The figures say 680 °C.
  - Testwork MET-53: 1.111 kg acid consumed and 0.470 kg left free per kg leached feed. So **about 30% of the dose is unreacted** and recoverable in principle. Acid tied up in gangue sulfates (Fe, Al, Mg, P) is recoverable only by high-temperature sulfate decomposition.
  - Raw testwork acid is about 459 kg per t feed (about 229.5 kt/yr) vs design net about 180 kt/yr (100%), implying about a 22% regeneration credit.
- **Regeneration as a service:** in the US, refinery spent alkylation acid is regenerated under cost pass-through contracts (Ecovyst). This shows a fee-based regeneration market exists on the Gulf Coast, but it is designed for refinery acid, not metal-sulfate-laden leach acid. [Substack](https://keithschaefer.substack.com/p/another-oddball-commodity-play-on); [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ecovyst-q2-earnings-call-highlights)
- **Intratec models** sulfuric acid production from spent acid by DCDA and by wet-gas sulfuric acid (WSA) process. Costs are paywalled. [Intratec – spent acid DCDA](https://www.intratec.us/analysis/sulfuric-acid-production-cost-sulfuric-acid-from-spent-acid-double-contact-process)

### Inferences
- **Two regeneration levels, two different economics:**
  - **(a) Free-acid recovery** (evaporation, salt crystallization, return to the bake) can at best recover about 30% of the gross dose. Steam from a sulfur burner could cover much of the evaporation energy.
  - **(b) Full sulfate roasting** (800–1,000 °C, SO2 to an acid plant) also recovers the acid tied up in iron and other sulfates. It needs a contact acid plant anyway, which strengthens the case for co-locating a sulfur burner (the same converter and absorption train can take both SO2 streams).
- **Cost of free-acid recovery.** At 5.9–15.2 GJ per t TiO2 and about 2.4–3.5 t acid per t TiO2, energy is roughly 2–6 GJ per t of acid recovered. At US$5–15/GJ that is about US$10–90/t of regenerated acid in energy alone, before capex. This rough range implies regeneration is clearly economic at 2026 acid prices (about US$350+) but marginal at 2023–2025 prices (about US$120–150).

### Gaps
- No published US$/t cost for regenerating sulfate-process spent acid or for filter-salt roasting was found.
- No published data was found on the 600–680 °C SO3-from-sulfate regeneration route assumed in the Tiros PEA. Its recycle percentage and energy need are unknown and must come from testwork.

## 7. Synthesis for modelling: acid cost per tonne under each option, and the price risk

### Takeaway
At late-October 2026 prices, making acid from sulfur costs roughly:
- **US$230–350/t acid at Brownsville** (Gulf liquid sulfur, about US$605–830/t);
- **US$320–425/t acid at Tiros** (imported sulfur at Brazil CFR about US$870–1,036 plus about 783 km of trucking).

Buying acid delivered costs about **US$380–420 (Brownsville)** and **US$400–460 (Tiros)**.

At 2023–2025 "normal" prices all options drop to about **US$70–220/t**. In those years, buying cheap imported or local by-product acid often beat building a small sulfur burner. The PEA's US$170/t delivered sits in that range.

The dominant risk is the sulfur price, which ranged more than 10× in 2020–2026. Texas has a structural sulfur-cost edge (domestic liquid sulfur surplus, no ocean freight, no Brazilian inland haul) of about **US$25–140/t of acid**, depending on market conditions. Shipping sulfur to the rock moves about 5× less tonnage than shipping rock to Texas.

### Cited Findings
- Input prices and freights are cited in sections 2–5 above. Plant cost, power and steam inputs are cited in section 1 above.

### Inferences (all calculated; formula and assumptions explicit)

**Formula.** Make-acid cost per t (100% H2SO4) = 0.327 × (delivered sulfur price) + non-sulfur operating cost (US$15–30) + capital charge (US$37–44 at about 180 kt/yr; 10% over 20 years) − power credit (US$13–26).

| Case (price date) | Delivered sulfur, US$/t S | Make acid, US$/t | Buy acid delivered, US$/t |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tiros, current spot** (29 Oct 2026: CFR US$860–880, plus US$40–70 inland, assumed) | 900–950 | **about 320–370** | about 400–460 (Brazil CFR acid US$350–370 Sep 2026, plus US$50–90 inland, assumed) |
| **Tiros, 4Q26 contract** (CFR US$1,036) | 1,076–1,106 | **about 380–425** | — |
| **Tiros, pre-war** (26 Feb 2026, CFR US$525) | 565–595 | **about 210–255** | — |

| **Brownsville, 2025** (Tampa US$116–310/lt, about US$114–305/t) | 135–355 | **about 70–175** | about 135–200 (SE US import CFR US$115–155, plus haul) |

**Readings from the table:**
- **Crisis behavior.** In a crisis, make and buy converge, because acid prices track sulfur. Owning a burner mainly buys supply security, about 6 MW of power, and steam for regeneration.
- **Calm-market behavior.** In calm markets, buying acid (especially by-product or idle-capacity acid) is often cheaper than a dedicated small burner, unless capital is already sunk.
- **Brazil vs Texas sulfur gap per t acid** = 0.327 × (Brazil CFR − Gulf price + inland Brazil haul − Texas haul):
  - about **US$25–45/t acid** using the Oct 2026 spot gap (US$45 between Brazil CFR and US Gulf FOB);
  - **about US$130–150/t acid** using the 4Q26 contract gap (Brazil US$1,036 vs Tampa US$605).
- **Price-risk band for Tiros at about 180 kt/yr acid (100%), about 187 kt/yr at 96%.** Each US$100/t change in sulfur moves acid cost about US$33/t, or about **US$6 M/yr**. Over the 2024 low (about US$50) to 2026 peak (about US$1,200) range, sulfur alone swings acid cost by about US$375/t, or about **US$70 M/yr**. That is about 64% of the PEA's total US$109.5 M/yr gross operating cost. (The opex figure is from the PEA supplementary release; the rest is calculated.)
- **Plant size.** Below about 100 kt/yr (about 300 t/d), the capital charge rises to about US$50–80/t. Such a plant is economic mainly when sulfur-versus-acid spreads are wide or supply security has high value (inference from the capex anchors in §1).

### Gaps
- Inland freight (Brazil and Texas), location factors for capex, and a current acid-plant EPC quote are assumed, not sourced. These are the largest open modelling inputs after sulfur price.
- Bulk-mineral ocean freight Santos → Brownsville, needed for the "move the rock" case, was not found.
