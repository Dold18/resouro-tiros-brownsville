# Research note 6: a Brownsville rare-earth module

*Tiros–Brownsville team · model v0.6 · 6 November 2026*

**Question:** Steve suggested that Brownsville makes financial sense as the place where Tiros's mixed rare-earth carbonate (MREC) is separated into light and heavy rare earths. What is Tiros's carbonate worth, what does separation cost, and who else would use a Brownsville plant?

**Short answer:** separating Tiros's own carbonate does not pay at any size or price we tested. Tiros's rare earths are mostly light, so separation adds only about US$7–13 per kg of oxide, roughly what it costs to run. A Brownsville plant pays only as a **hub**: one that also processes other producers' carbonate for a fee, with government support. The numbers are in the model's RE module tab.

Status labels follow the brief: *Known* (published), *Assumption* (our choice), *Needs testing*.

## 1. What is in Tiros's carbonate

The PEA does not publish the element make-up of the carbonate. It says only that NdPr, terbium and dysprosium carry 84% of the US$57.40/kg basket value (PEA p.348). We built a split from what the PEA does report:

| Evidence | What it says | Where |
|---|---|---|
| BK19 head assay | CeO₂ 3,700 ppm, La₂O₃ 1,900, Nd₂O₃ 1,600, Pr₆O₁₁ 600, of 9,116 ppm TREO | PEA p.197–199, Tables 13.5–13.6 |
| BK09 groups | Heavy rare earths 200 of 5,000 ppm (4%) | PEA p.192, Table 13.2 |
| Mineral resource | Magnet oxides (Nd, Pr, Tb, Dy) are 26–28% of TREO in every domain | PEA p.44, Table 1.1 |
| First auger results | Nd+Pr oxide averaged 20% of TREO | [Resouro release](https://www.newsfilecorp.com/release/180375/Resouro-Reports-High-Grade-Initial-Results-at-Tiros-Rare-Earths-and-Titanium-Project-in-Brazil) |
| PEA basket | NdPr + Dy + Tb = 84% of US$57.40 = US$48.2/kg at NdPr US$157, Dy US$1,640, Tb US$3,609 | PEA p.348 |

**Our split** *(Needs testing)*:

| Oxide | Share of TREO |
|---|---|
| Cerium | 44.5% |
| Lanthanum | 22.0% |
| NdPr | 26.6% |
| Samarium | 2.5% |
| Yttrium | 1.5% |
| Gadolinium | 1.2% |
| Other heavies (Ho, Er, Tm, Yb, Lu) | 0.84% |
| Europium | 0.5% |
| Dysprosium | 0.30% |
| Terbium | 0.06% |

- **Dysprosium and terbium** were chosen so that NdPr + Dy + Tb is worth US$48.9/kg at PEA prices, against the PEA's US$48.2.
- **The full basket** at our prices is US$51.5/kg against the PEA's US$57.4. The PEA does not explain how it values the other 16% of its basket. At market prices, cerium, lanthanum and the minor heavies are worth only about US$2.7/kg.
- **What it means:** Tiros is a light rare-earth deposit. NdPr is about three-quarters of the value. Dysprosium and terbium are under 0.4% of the oxide: about 0.9 t of Dy and 0.15 t of Tb a year at the brief's 5,000 t/month.

**Why the outside-China case does not help Tiros much:** NdPr is cheaper outside China (US$115) than in the PEA (US$157). The heavy premium applies to only 0.36% of Tiros's oxide.

**Competition for feed:** Ucore (Louisiana), Aclara (Louisiana), MP Materials' heavy circuit (~3,000 t/yr of feed), Energy Fuels (Utah), ReElement (Indiana) and Lynas (Malaysia) all want carbonate. Lynas says its heavy capacity (240 t/yr Dy, 50 t/yr Tb) is limited by feed, not by plant ([Lynas call, Aug 2026](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-lynas-rare-earths-posts-record-pricing-in-q4-2026-as-stock-falls-93CH-4804573)). Competition for feed pushes toll fees down.

**What a producer would pay.** A producer only tolls if it gains more than by selling carbonate. Our calculation for a Caldeira-like carbonate (NdPr 31%, Dy+Tb about 1%):
- Stage 1 gains it about US$6–11/kg.
- Stage 2 gains it about US$10–19/kg, depending on prices.

Heavy-rich clay carbonates gain far more. Serra Verde's basket is about US$190/kg at outside-China prices, which is why Aclara can charge US$40/kg. The model uses toll fees of US$10 (Stage 1) and US$20 (Stage 2) *(Assumption)*.

## 7. Results (model v0.6, 5,000 t/month, PEA long-term prices)

The v0.6 model groups the ten oxides above into five groups: NdPr, dysprosium, terbium, other heavies and lanthanum + cerium. It keeps two price cases, PEA and outside China. The China-market and US-floor cases gave results between those two in v0.5. Grants and the 45X credit are now one "government support" input, 25% of build cost. Stage 1's build cost is now Ucore's US$135M for about 3,600 t/yr, scaled with the six-tenths rule.

| Set-up | Other producers' oxide | Net present value before tax | Toll fee needed to break even |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stage 1, Tiros only | 0 | −US$54M | – |
| Stage 1 as a hub | 4,000 t/yr at US$10/kg | −US$36M | US$10.9/kg |
| Stage 2, full separation hub | 4,000 t/yr at US$20/kg | US$102M | US$17.4/kg |

**What moves the answer most** (Stage 2 hub):

| Change | Net present value |
|---|---|
| Other producers' feed: 2,000 t/yr | −US$31M |
| Other producers' feed: 8,000 t/yr | US$396M |
| Other producers' feed: 12,000 t/yr | US$708M |
| No government support | US$32M |
| Aclara's October 2026 cost basis (US$494M) | −US$62M |
| Running cost US$3/kg higher | −US$29M |
| Fee US$15/kg | −US$95M |
| Fee US$25/kg | US$298M |

**Separating only Tiros's carbonate, at full PEA size** (3,636 t/yr): −US$33M (Stage 1) and −US$177M (Stage 2) at PEA prices. Worse at outside-China prices.

## 8. Open questions

1. The actual element make-up and purity of Tiros carbonate. Thorium and uranium in it decide licensing.
2. Whether Caldeira, Serra Verde or others would sign toll agreements, and at what fee.
3. A Texas wastewater route for a separation plant (chloride and ammonium or sodium salts). Lynas's Seadrift plant failed on this.
4. Grant and loan availability for a Brownsville plant.
